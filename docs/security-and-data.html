Security and data, written against us

Everything below is a gap, not a feature. It is written the way a hostile reviewer would find it — the worst honest reading first, then what actually bounds it, then what would close it. Where a number appears, it was read out of the live database, not estimated.

Verified against fayr_dev · 24 August 2026

If you read one thing

On three of our seven marketplaces we pay for a review that no other shopper can ever read.

Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart have no public review page. What we verify there is a rating marked on the order inside the buyer's own account. That is a deliberate decision about two launch partners and one follow-on, taken knowingly — but it means the product's stated purpose, an incentivised review, is not what is happening on those three. Amazon and Flipkart are the platforms where a public review genuinely exists, is fetched, and is re-checked afterwards.

Open holes

Things that would cost money, or embarrass us, today.

01Open

Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart pay for a rating with no public review anywhere

What is true
Those three readers set the review as published from the order's own rating marker — the marketplace saying "this account rated this item". None of the three publishes a review page, a permalink, or review text a stranger can read. So there is nothing to fetch, nothing to re-check later, and nothing to show anyone who asks to see the review.
Live right now: the Blinkit task in its holding period, the Zepto task at the review stage and the Instamart task at delivery are all marked publicly reviewed on that basis. Three of the twelve tasks on the demo account.
What a hostile reviewer says
You are paying people to tap a star in a private app. Show me one of these reviews. You cannot, because it does not exist anywhere a customer would see it — so what exactly is the advertiser buying?
What actually bounds it
Nothing technical. The purchase itself is verified from the marketplace's own order record, the return window still runs, and the refund is still gated on the amount — so we are not paying for a purchase that did not happen. We are paying for a signal weaker than the one the product name implies.
Also worth admitting
Until this week the code contradicted itself about this. A comment in the Task screen said that rating marker "must never stand as proof on its own", while the reader accepted it and paid; and the app told those users "a Fayr reviewer confirms your rating" when no reviewer was involved and none was waited for. Both have been corrected to say what happens. The behaviour was left alone on purpose — Blinkit and Zepto are in the demo, and changing what they pay on today would break it.
What would close it
Either pay only where a public review exists — which removes those three platforms from the product as described — or reclassify them as rating campaigns, price them differently, and stop calling the result a review. That is a commercial decision, not an engineering one.
02Open

A Meesho payout rests on one person's word, and nothing can ever re-check it

What is true
Meesho shows the star on the order and keeps the written review inside its own app. No machine can establish whether a Meesho review is publicly visible — not the scraper, not the server. So a SUPPORT member opens the product page by hand and records that they saw it, and that record is what starts the holding period.
And it can never be revisited. Meesho publishes no review permalink, so the holding-period visibility re-check — our countermeasure against a review deleted after payout — has nothing to fetch. A Meesho review taken down the day after the refund lands is unrecoverable and invisible.
What a hostile reviewer says
One support account can turn an unpayable task into a payable one by asserting something no system of yours can check, then or ever. Show me the review from six months ago.
What actually bounds it
The marketplace's own star has to be on file first, so a person cannot invent the review itself — only confirm the visibility of one the marketplace already reported. Anything that outranks a person refuses the action: if a machine has checked a public page, either way, the assertion is rejected rather than layered on top. The page URL and the date are required in both directions, the written note cannot be blank, the decision is audited with the previous value beside it, and it can be withdrawn only by the tier that made it. No money moves on it: the return window, the amount and the finance-gated withdrawal all still stand between it and a rupee.
What would close it
Nothing available to us. Meesho would have to publish a per-review address. Until then this is a hole we operate inside deliberately, and the card in the staff console says so on its face — not only in this document.
03Open

The one action that actually moves money out is the one that is not in the audit log

What is true
Approving a withdrawal and marking it paid write the deciding staff id, the timestamp and the bank UTR onto the withdrawal row. They write nothing to the admin audit log that every other staff action uses.
Read out of the live database: two withdrawals have been paid, and the audit log holds zero rows for either of them. It holds 7 rows for staff viewing a screenshot, 53 for staff logging in, and 40 for campaign status changes.
What a hostile reviewer says
You log a support agent looking at a picture, and you do not log the person releasing money out of the company.
What actually bounds it
The record is not absent, it is in the wrong place: the withdrawal row names who decided it, when, and the UTR of the transfer. What is missing is the single chronological trail — so "what did this staff member do last month" answers correctly for six kinds of action and silently omits the one that matters most.
What would close it
Two audit calls in the withdrawal service, matching the pattern the other five staff actions already use. Small, and found while writing this document rather than by anybody asking.
04Open

Two routes to "money that left Fayr", and they cannot agree by construction

What is true
The Reports screen's paid-out total sums the withdrawals table where the status is paid: ₹3,787.40. The double-entry ledger's payout account holds ₹18,282.40 across 13 entries. Both claim to describe money leaving the company.
The reason they differ is structural, not a bug to be found. Money is reserved out of the user's wallet at request time — a real ledger movement from the user to the payout account — and marking a withdrawal paid posts no ledger entry at all. So the payout account accumulates every reservation and never discharges, and no bank UTR is ever linked to a ledger movement.
What a hostile reviewer says
Which of these two numbers is the money that left? Neither, and you cannot reconcile them, so you cannot audit your own disbursements.
What actually bounds it
The ledger itself is sound: it is append-only, double-entry, in whole paise, with a database trigger that refuses deletes, and every individual entry is balanced. Nothing here suggests money went missing — the user-side balances and the house account are consistent. What is missing is the discharging leg that would make "paid" a ledger fact rather than a row status.
What would close it
Post the payout account's discharge when a transfer is marked paid, and carry the UTR on that entry. Left alone on purpose for now: it is a money change and it wants doing carefully, not before a demo.
05Open

Nothing prevents one person holding SUPPORT and ADMIN — ADMIN already is every role

What is true
A staff account has exactly one role, so nobody has to hold two. ADMIN is a super-role: the authorization guard passes every role check for it outright. One ADMIN account can therefore state what one unit cost, confirm the review is publicly live, clear the duplicate-order hold, approve the withdrawal, and mark it paid — the entire path from "no amount known" to "money gone", alone.
There are five staff accounts on the dev database and two of them are ADMIN. The demo runs entirely as one of them, because one login reaches every queue.
What a hostile reviewer says
Your separation of duties is a comment in a controller. One account does the whole chain, and one of the two steps it takes is not even logged.
What actually bounds it
For the three amount-and-review decisions: a real ceiling derived from the campaign price and the order's own total, a required source from a closed list, a note that cannot be blank, an audit row naming the account and the previous value, and a refusal to overwrite anything a machine established. Those are genuine and they are enforced server-side, not in the panel. But they bound the figure, not the concentration: an ADMIN doing every step in sequence breaks no rule.
What would close it
Make the release of money refuse a staff member who decided any input to it — a four-eyes rule on the account, not on the role. And stop ADMIN being an implicit superset of FINANCE.
06Open

Consent is not required in order to be paid — and already has not been

What the consent record DOES cover
Which version of the terms was agreed to, and when. The version is mandatory — accepting without one is refused, because a record with no version cannot show what was agreed. The timestamp is set on the server, never taken from the client. Sending "I do not accept" is ignored rather than treated as a withdrawal, which would be a separate deliberate act.
What it does NOT cover
It is not required to participate. Nothing in the claim path, the evidence path or the refund path looks at it. A user can claim an offer, buy, be verified and be paid having never accepted anything. This is not hypothetical: of 33 users on the dev database, exactly one has a terms record — and the account with two refunded tasks and a paid withdrawal of ₹590.40 is not that one.
There is no history. The version and the timestamp are columns on the user row, so accepting a new version overwrites the old record. Nothing shows that someone ever accepted the previous terms.
There is no text. Only a version string is stored. Nothing pins that string to the words it refers to, so if the document's wording changes without the version changing, the record points at nothing checkable.
There is no context. No IP address, no device, no user agent — nothing that would distinguish the account holder accepting from somebody else on their phone.
It is skippable by design. Every setup screen offers "Do this later", including the last one, which is where consent is written. The demo run-sheet now calls that out as a do-not.
There is no way to withdraw it. Not built, and not designed.
What a hostile reviewer says
You have paid a user who never agreed to your terms, you cannot produce the text they would have agreed to, and you could not prove a prior acceptance if one existed.
What would close it
Gate the claim on a current acceptance; store consents append-only with the document's own hash; and record enough context to tie an acceptance to a session. The first of those is a few lines and changes user behaviour, so it is a product decision.
07Open

Screenshots are stored privately and never deleted

What is true
Uploaded screenshots are handled as private data and they are handled properly: written outside the public uploads directory, reachable only through a role-checked streaming endpoint, and every view is audited (there are 7 such audit rows on dev). The storage layer has a delete method written for retention purging.
Nothing calls it. There is no scheduled purge and no retention period enforced anywhere, so an uploaded screenshot is kept until somebody removes it by hand. The dev database happens to hold none right now, which is a coincidence of an earlier reset rather than a policy working.
What a hostile reviewer says
You correctly identified these images as sensitive, built the delete function, and then kept every one of them forever.
What actually bounds it
Access is genuinely restricted, and the image hash and the case row are designed to outlive the bytes — so a purge would not destroy the fraud signal (the same image appearing on several accounts). The machinery for doing this right exists; the schedule does not.
What would close it
One job on the existing maintenance cron, and a retention period written down as policy rather than implied.

Known limits

Deliberate, priced in, and worth saying before someone asks.

08Limit

Meesho cannot be paid end to end at all yet

What is true
Two separate things stop it, and the eyes-on-page confirmation only removes one. Meesho publishes no delivery date — the order carries a status word, not a date — and the return window is anchored to a date, so a Meesho task cannot reach the delivered stage or start its hold. And Meesho gives no amount of any kind, not even an order total, so every Meesho refund would additionally wait on a staff amount decision.
On top of that, Meesho web login is currently blocked by an experiment on their side, so no Meesho order history has ever actually been captured.
Why it was left
Closing it would mean a fourth privileged staff action — a person typing a delivery date, which sets the return-window clock and therefore the date money becomes payable. That is a bigger power than any of the three that exist, and it was not built rather than being built quietly.
09Limit

No DLT registration, so we can send one-time codes and nothing else

What is true
India requires DLT registration — a company entity and paperwork — before transactional or promotional SMS can be sent on a registered header. We have none. Today's texts are one-time login codes the user has just asked for, on a trial account, and the route can be suspended without notice.
What it means in practice
No marketing messages, no reminders, no notifications by SMS at all until the registration exists. Presenting that as a deliberate limit is honest; presenting it as a feature would not be. It is paperwork and a company entity, not engineering.
10Limit

The SMS account is a trial: five verified numbers, and thirty days

What is true
A trial account delivers only to numbers verified inside that account, at most five of them ever, and the trial expires thirty days after it was opened. There is no way to add a recipient on the spot.
What it means in practice
A code cannot be sent to anyone whose number is not already on the list — so no live sign-in for a visitor, a director, or a second device. The thirty-day clock also means a demo that works this week may need the account renewing before it works again. The demo run-sheet leads with this rather than discovering it on stage.
11Limit

PAN and payment details are stored as plain text, and a PAN cannot be corrected

What is true
A PAN is required before a withdrawal and is anchored to exactly one account — that is the multi-account countermeasure, and it works: a PAN already used elsewhere is refused, and UPI ids and bank accounts are deduplicated across users the same way. On dev, 12 of 33 users have a PAN and there are 12 payout methods with 12 distinct UPI ids.
They are stored as ordinary columns. There is no application-level encryption — protection is whatever the database and the disk provide. And because anchoring is deliberately one-way, a user who mistypes their PAN cannot fix it from the app; it needs someone with database access.
What a hostile reviewer says
You hold a permanent government identifier and a bank account for every paying user, in plain text, with no key management and no way for the user to correct a typo.
12Limit

The Meesho tests are built from our own code, not from a real account

What is true
Every Meesho test fixture is shaped from the emitter's own source in our scraper, because Meesho web login is blocked by an experiment on their side and no Meesho order history has ever been captured. So the tests prove the reader behaves consistently with what we believe the payload looks like — not that the payload looks like that.
It is stated at the top of the fixture in the repository, so nobody later mistakes it for captured data. Every other platform's fixtures are shaped from verified real captures, with the personal ones deliberately not committed.
What a hostile reviewer says
Your Meesho support is tested against your own assumptions. The first real account you connect could disagree with all of it.

Not built

Say these first rather than being caught by them.

  • Account deletion. There is no delete route anywhere in the backend. A user cannot remove themselves and we cannot remove them through the product.
  • Data export. Nothing produces a copy of what we hold on a person.
  • Consent withdrawal. Refusing the terms is ignored, not recorded (06).
  • Retention purging. The delete function exists and nothing calls it (07).
  • Reconciliation of disbursements. Two figures, no discharging ledger leg (04).
  • Four-eyes on a payout. One ADMIN account can do the whole chain (05).
Every figure here was read from the live dev database on 24 August 2026 — the two paid withdrawals, the payout account's balance, the audit-log counts, the consent counts and the PAN counts included. Where something could not be verified against real data, that is said in the finding rather than smoothed over.
