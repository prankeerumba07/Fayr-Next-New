Checking Fayr yourself

This page assumes nobody is available to help you and no assistant has any credit left. Everything below is two commands and a list of things to look at. You do not need to understand any of it. You need to be able to type two short things and know what a working screen looks like.

Written 27 August 2026. Both commands on this page were run from start to finish before it was written, including on purpose with something broken, to check they explain themselves.

Before you start

You need three things open or available.

  • The Terminal app on your Mac. It is in Applications, then Utilities. It is the window where you type the two commands.
  • Your phone on the same wifi as this laptop. Not mobile data. The same wifi.
  • The Expo Go app on your phone, if you have an Android phone. On an iPhone you use the normal Camera app instead.

Start everything: one command

Open Terminal and type these two lines, pressing return after each.

cd ~/FAYR-Next ./start

That is it. It takes about a minute. It will start the database if it is not running, bring it up to date, put the practice data in, start the Fayr server, put the staff panel on the network, and start the app for your phone.

What you should see

Nine numbered steps, each ending in a green word: done. Then a box that says EVERYTHING IS RUNNING, followed by three addresses. Then a large square black and white pattern.

If any step goes wrong it stops there, in red, saying which step failed and what to try. It will never just stop with nothing.

Open the staff panel on your laptop

In the box it printed, find the line under THE STAFF PANEL, ON THIS LAPTOP. Copy the whole address, including everything after the question mark, and paste it into Chrome or Safari.

The part after the question mark is not decoration. It tells the panel which copy of Fayr to talk to. Without it the panel talks to the other copy, the one being shown on Monday, and everything will look subtly wrong.

What you should see

A sign in box. Use a staff email and password. The start command prints all four of them, one for each team, at the step where it puts the practice data in. Scroll up in the Terminal window to find them.

Open the staff panel on your phone

Find the line under THE STAFF PANEL, ON YOUR PHONE. It has numbers in it rather than a word. Type that whole address into your phone browser, or send it to yourself in a message and tap it.

If your phone says it cannot reach it, the cause is almost always one of two things: your phone is on mobile data instead of wifi, or your phone is on a different wifi network from the laptop.

Open the app on your phone

Look at the large square black and white pattern in the Terminal window.

  • iPhone: open the Camera app and point it at the pattern. A banner appears at the top. Tap it.
  • Android: open Expo Go, choose Scan QR code, and point it at the pattern.

The Fayr app opens on your phone. It is talking to this copy of Fayr, not the Monday one. The start command sets that up for you every time.

Check everything: one command

Stop the running one first by pressing Control and C together in the Terminal window. Then type this.

./check

It runs every check in the whole project. It takes about three minutes and it does not stop at the first problem, because if two things are broken you want to know about both.

What you should see

Five areas, each with a green all good, and then a green box saying ALL 5 AREAS ARE GOOD, with the numbers beneath it, and the sentence: Nothing is broken. It is safe to show this to somebody.

If something is wrong you get a red box instead, naming which area, what that area means in ordinary words, and the first few failing checks. The last thing on screen is always a sentence, never a wall of output.

One thing to know before you worry. There is a known flaw in the area called the backend end to end. It fails roughly one run in four for reasons that have nothing to do with the code. The way to tell is the phrase socket hang up in the detail. If that is what it says, run ./check again, and if it passes then that was all it was. Anything else is real.

The new things to look at, and what working looks like

Chat with us, in the app

On your phone: tap My Profile at the bottom, then Chat with us at the top of the list.

Type what are tickets and send it. Then try mera refund kab aayega. Then try something silly like do you deliver to Kathmandu.

Working looks like: the first two get a real answer, and the Hindi one answers in Hindi written with English letters. The silly one comes back in an orange bubble with a small label saying Waiting for a person. After every reply it asks Did that help, with two buttons.

One thing that will surprise you: at first nothing gets answered at all. That is correct. Every answer starts as a draft and a person has to approve it, which is the next thing on this list.

The answer book, in the staff panel

Sign in as the customer support account. In the left hand list, under Customer support, choose Answer book.

Working looks like: seventy five answers, twenty five subjects in three languages. Every one says Waiting for approval and Drafted by the assistant. Each one also carries a line saying no person has read it yet, and the Hindi ones add that nobody who speaks the language has checked the wording.

Pick one, read it, and press Approve and make live. Then go back to Chat with us on your phone and ask about it. It answers now, immediately. That is the whole point of the screen.

Try Correct the words on a live answer, change a word, and save. It stays live and the change is there the next time somebody asks. Now try writing a new answer and put a technical word in the body, one a shopper would never say out loud. It saves, and it warns you, and it refuses to be approved until you fix it.

Chat questions, in the staff panel

Under Customer support, choose Chat questions.

Working looks like: a row of numbers across the top, then the questions you asked from your phone, in your own words, with which answer was used and how well it matched. The silly one you asked sits under Waiting. Press Close on it and it leaves the queue.

Live pages, in the staff panel

Sign in as the operations account instead. Under Front end operations, choose Live pages.

Working looks like: it says nobody has run this yet, and tells you how. Most offers have no shop page saved, and it says so as a number rather than pretending everything is fine.

To run it: on your phone, My Profile, then Check offer pages. Sign in with the operations staff email and password. Press the button. Then reload the Live pages screen on the laptop and the findings are there.

A greyed out offer, in the app

This is the one worth seeing. After running the live page check, if any offer came back as ended or sold out, go to Home in the app.

Working looks like: that offer is still in the list, not missing. It is faded, with an orange strip across the top saying what happened and that it may come back, and the button says Not right now instead of Claim.

It never disappears. That is deliberate. Somebody who saw it yesterday has to be able to find it and see why.

The offer check, in the staff panel

Under Front end operations, choose Offer check. This is the older one that reads only Fayr's own records, so it needs nothing from a phone.

Working looks like: thirteen offers looked at, one blocking problem, and a list. It finds the offer with no picture, and the two offers sharing one picture. It also says clearly what it cannot check rather than counting that as a pass.

When something does not start

Work down this list. Stop when it works.

  • It said which step failed. Read what it told you to try. That text was written for exactly that failure and it is more specific than anything on this page.
  • It mentions Docker. Open the Docker app from Applications by hand. Wait until the whale icon in the top menu bar stops moving. Then run ./start again.
  • The app on the phone says it cannot reach Fayr. Your phone is on mobile data, or on a different wifi. Fix that and shake the phone to reload, or close and reopen the app.
  • The panel says it cannot reach the Fayr server. You probably pasted the address without the part after the question mark. Go back to the Terminal window and copy the whole line.
  • Something is still running from last time. Close the Terminal window entirely and open a new one, then start again.
  • You want to see exactly what a part said. Everything is written down. In Finder, press Command Shift G and go to ~/FAYR-Next/.local-logs. One file per part.

Two things to be careful about

This is for checking on your own machine. The start command deliberately makes Fayr reachable from your phone across your wifi, which is what lets you check the app for real. That is right here and would be wrong on a real server. It does not switch off any sign in and it does not remove any security protection. It does mean anything else on your wifi can reach the address while it is running, so do not leave it running on a network you do not know.

The other copy is untouched. Everything on this page happens in the folder called FAYR-Next, on its own database and its own port. The copy being shown on Monday is a different folder and nothing here can reach it. The start command writes down which copy your phone talks to, every single time, because pointing at the wrong one is the easiest mistake to make and the hardest to notice.

If you get stuck on something this page does not cover, the thing to write down is what you typed and the last five lines you saw. That is almost always enough for somebody to tell you what happened.
